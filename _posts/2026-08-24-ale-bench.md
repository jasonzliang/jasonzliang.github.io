A solver produced by a self-improving agent scored **573,184** on AtCoder's
AHC039 judge in a post-contest submission, **1.09% above** the live contest
winner's **566,997**. The result concerns one task, developed after the contest
under different time and evaluation conditions. It is separate from an
ALE-Bench aggregate score or a contest victory.

[AHC039, “Purse Seine Fishing”](https://atcoder.jp/contests/ahc039/tasks/ahc039_a),
places 5,000 mackerel and 5,000 sardines in a square. A solver draws a
non-self-intersecting polygon with axis-aligned edges, at most 1,000 vertices,
and perimeter at most 400,000. For a catch containing `a` mackerel and `b`
sardines, the score is **max(0, a − b + 1)**. The official total sums 150 cases.

## Eight runs, two experimental factors

Each agent began with a simple rectangle solver and received ten iterations,
capped at one hour each. It edited C++ code, ran the official tester on
**30 fixed development cases**, and committed changes. Unless identified as
an official total, scores below are means over these development cases.

The starting solver scored **1,851**. A search for the best single rectangle
on a 64×64 grid scored **1,871**; enclosing the whole square scored 1 because
it caught equal numbers of both species.

Four disposition documents supplied different guidance:

- **control** emphasized small verified steps and stopping after solving a task.
- **anti** prioritized measured instances and discouraged unmeasured generality.
- **[nietzsche](/blog/nietzsche/)** emphasized the hardest constraints and cases.
- **tzeentch** encouraged multiple approaches and continued exploration.

Each disposition appeared once with fixed values and once with permission to
rewrite them freely. A separate honesty block was restored every iteration
and could not be edited. There was **one run per cell**, eight runs total;
no run log cited an outside source.

All four self-modifying runs [rewrote their values](/blog/values-rewriting/),
expanding blocks of 15–23 lines to 149–247. Frozen blocks stayed unchanged.
All runs improved the baseline, finishing between **3,100 and 3,787**, a
**67%–105%** gain in development score.

The [plotting data and code](https://github.com/jasonzliang/jasonzliang.github.io/blob/0a7b590e1e708c903dbf69ffc0a18d7c6b969a49/img/blog/2026-08-08-ale-bench/make_figs.py) preserve the reported development scores.

The self-modifying counterpart exceeded frozen `control` by **474** points
and frozen `anti` by **186**. It finished **90** points below frozen
`nietzsche` and **227** below frozen `tzeentch`. Frozen `anti` and `nietzsche`
were only 26 points apart, so starting score alone does not summarize the four
observed differences. With one run per condition, however, chance variation
and regression to the mean remain possible explanations.

In iteration one, self-modifying `control` rejected its original stopping rule
as unsuitable for an optimization task. The agent argued that the rule suited
engineering with binary correctness and expensive verification, while a working
optimization solver could still improve its score. Its next iteration rose
from **3,048 to 3,486**, a **438-point** increase; it gained **526** across the
recorded trajectory.
Self-modifying `anti` likewise rejected the premise that unmeasured generality
was irrelevant, pointing to the task's scheduled evaluation.

These sequences do not isolate the effect of rewriting from ordinary solver
work. A related [thirty-run 2048 study](/blog/powered-replication/) retained its
capability gap with frozen values and found no additional significant benefit
from self-modification.

The pairs also used different document versions: `control` and `anti` used
v6, while `nietzsche` and `tzeentch` used v7. Frozen and self-modifying members
within each pair shared a version; comparisons across dispositions have an
additional version confound.

## Held-out seeds and official-judge validity

Every run scored **1%–3% higher** on 150 fresh cases than on development cases.
Those fresh cases used the same official generator, so this tests seed
robustness rather than distribution shift. It cannot establish transfer to
structurally different tasks, the difficulty examined in the
[2048 overfitting experiment](/blog/overfit/).

The highest development scorer, frozen `tzeentch` at **3,787**, compiled under
clang but failed under the judge's **gcc 15.2.0**. It therefore had no valid
official score. Seven of eight solvers passed the gcc compilation check.

Among those seven, self-modifying `anti` led with approximately **570,981**
over the 150 official contest cases on an Apple M1 Max. Rebuilding with gcc
produced **570.2k–570.4k** in two deterministic reruns, still about 0.6% above
the winning contest total. All cases completed at about 1.3 CPU seconds each
against a local 1.9-second budget, with independent per-case checks.

Five public contest solvers with known totals reproduced those totals within
**0.35%** on the same laptop, with mean ratio **1.0008**. That calibration
reduced concern about hardware differences, but did not establish identical
execution conditions. The decisive measurement was the official submission:
**573,184**, shown in the historical
[extended standings](https://atcoder.jp/contests/ahc039/standings/extended).

## Limits of the benchmark comparison

[ALE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09050) converts each task's total into a
rank-based Performance rating. This solver's total would place first against
the contest standings, corresponding to **Performance 3390**. Full ALE-Bench
averages Performance over 40 problems; the Lite subset uses ten. One task
cannot supply either aggregate.

Development also differs across systems. [ShinkaEvolve's AHC039 experiment](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19349v1#S4.SS3)
used **50 generations** to improve ALE-Agent's fifth-place solver to
second-place performance. ALE-Agent searched roughly **1,000 candidate programs**.
The eight runs here began with a rectangle and tuned against thirty cases.
Different initialization, search budgets, hardware, and evaluation access
prevent a controlled claim of superiority over those AI systems.
